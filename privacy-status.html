Privacy progress: steps toward COPPA and FERPA compliance

As of September 6, 2026

This dated status notice covers My First Volo, Story Builder, Primo Volo, and Morphology. It describes implemented protections and the limits of verification to date. It is not a completed Privacy Policy, a compliance certification, or a guarantee against a data breach.

Use student nicknames or labels, not real names. Choose a made-up label such as Owl 7. Do not enter student names, initials, school student IDs, email addresses, birth dates, or identifying details in learner labels, class names, stories, or instructional notes. Keep any list linking labels to real students securely outside First Volo.

Implemented protections · representative checks completed

  • Full products use educator account access and product authorization. Students access the full products using a Class Code and Student Code issued by their teacher. Student sign-in does not ask for a name, email address, birth date, or a self-created password. Product access is determined by their class.
  • Database access controls restrict student records by authorized educator and student context. Protected progress reports perform additional access checks.
  • Sign-out controls end sessions. Student labels, saved activities, and teaching records remain protected data even when real names are omitted.
  • The public Story Builder sample uses browser saving and downloadable story files rather than individual student accounts. The full products also use browser storage and, where enabled, account-linked cloud records.
  • Student-label and writing guidance across the products discourages identifying information. Guidance does not automatically detect or remove identifying text.

Checks completed

Recorded checks include student-to-student and educator-to-educator isolation, protected report access, and representative signed-in and signed-out product journeys. September 6 browser checks covered an entitled educator, an educator without product access, and an authorized QA student across the three products, including a restricted Primo educator page.

These checks are representative. They are not an independent security audit or a test of every role, route, device, and browser state. Follow-up checks remain for some return-to-product journeys and recently corrected sign-out behavior.

Steps toward COPPA compliance

We are using the FTC’s six-step compliance plan (May 2026) to organize the remaining work. None of the six steps is marked fully complete by this status notice.

  1. Assess coverage and collection — in progress. A data inventory has been drafted. Verify each product’s collection, hosting logs, persistent identifiers, integrations, and any voice-related features; omitting names alone does not settle COPPA coverage.
  2. Publish a complete privacy policy — draft prepared. Reconcile actual data practices, operators/service providers, purposes, disclosures, retention, and rights procedures before final publication. This status page does not replace that policy.
  3. Provide direct notice — not yet verified. Prepare and verify delivery of the required notice before covered collection.
  4. Establish valid consent or school authorization — not yet verified. A school-authorized model for under-13 use has been selected, but the authorization process and evidence have not been verified. An educator login or access request alone is not proof of valid authorization.
  5. Support ongoing rights — procedure drafted. Verify requestor identity, access/review, withdrawal of consent, and deletion through a tested process.
  6. Security, retention, and deletion — partially implemented. Access controls and selected tests are in place. Complete the written security program, provider assurances, risk review, and verified retention and deletion operations.

The FTC permits school authorization only within the qualifying educational context; the operator remains responsible for COPPA. We must verify that our process satisfies those conditions. See FTC guidance on COPPA and schools.

Steps toward supporting FERPA-compliant school use

Relevant regulations include 34 CFR Part 99, particularly §§ 99.31(a)(1) and 99.33 when relying on the school-official exception.

FERPA principally governs covered educational agencies and institutions. A service provider must meet the conditions of the school’s lawful disclosure arrangement. We are reviewing the Department of Education’s vendor guidance; use is not automatically FERPA-compliant because an educator creates an account.

  • School authority and control — not yet verified. Document the applicable disclosure basis, authorized purposes, legitimate educational interest, school control over use and maintenance, and restrictions on reuse and redisclosure.
  • Contract terms — draft prepared. Review school agreements and service-provider terms, including access/correction support, data handling, termination, and deletion.
  • Access restrictions — implemented with partial testing. Expand checks and document school-directed procedures for record access and correction.

Official checklists guiding the work

  • FTC COPPA Rule and current rule materials — legal source, alongside the six-step plan above.
  • Department of Education: Model Terms of Service checklist — review collection, use, sharing, and contractual terms.
  • Department of Education: Data Security Checklist — review governance and the security program.

These are official guidance resources, not certificates or completed audits of First Volo. Our status labels reflect the evidence available as of September 6; they do not postpone requirements that already apply. Applicable state laws and individual school requirements also require review. Legal review remains outstanding.

What is still being verified

  • Operational retention, backup restoration, and permanent deletion behavior. Draft retention periods must not yet be treated as verified technical guarantees.
  • Final Privacy Policy and school/district data-protection documents, including formal COPPA and FERPA review.
  • Additional security and accessibility review, including dependency/application scans, review of identifying information in logs and error messages, and broader browser testing.
  • Remaining account-security work, including leaked-password protection and mailbox two-factor authentication.

Limits to understand

Students do not need to supply real names or personal details to use the learning activities. Story Builder’s open-text fields are intended for fictional story generation using made-up characters and details, not student personal information. The products can store learning activity, account-linked records, and fictional text users enter. The fields do not automatically detect identifying information entered contrary to that guidance.

Using a made-up student label reduces direct identification but does not make records anonymous. Story content, class context, or other details may still identify a student. Labels do not replace security controls or privacy obligations.

Products are hosted on static websites. Account gates control the normal product experience, while database access controls protect student records. Static curriculum files such as HTML, images, and PDFs are not confidential merely because a page requires sign-in in the browser.
